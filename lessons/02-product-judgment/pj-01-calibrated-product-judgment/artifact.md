# Artifact template · Calibrated product view

The tutor fills this in with the learner, using the learner's judgment and the
learner's evidence. Never invent a number, a quote, or a source. Where something
is unknown, write `<unknown>` and carry it into the Gaps section. A named gap is
a finding. A fabricated one is a liability.

Confidence is the learner's to state, not the tutor's to assign. If the learner
states a confidence the evidence does not support, say so and let them change
it.

Delete the guidance in parentheses in the finished artifact.

```markdown
# Calibrated product view · <short name of the position>

- **Date:** <YYYY-MM-DD>
- **Product:** <product>
- **Decision this position answers:** <the decision statement it takes a side of>
- **Owner of the consequence:** <person or role>
- **Specialist authority:** <who holds expertise this position depends on>

## Position

<One sentence. A choice from the live alternatives, not a topic and not a
direction. "Build X this quarter" is a position. "Explore X" is not.>

**Alternatives displaced by this position:**

1. <option not being taken>
2. <option not being taken — including "no change yet" where that is real>

## Inputs

Mark the row this position mostly rests on.

| Input | What it contributes here | Load-bearing? |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence** | <what was observed> | <yes / partly / no> |
| **Accumulated exposure** | <the pattern, and where you saw it before> | <yes / partly / no> |
| **Strategic context** | <what the company is currently trying to buy> | <yes / partly / no> |
| **Accountability** | <who carries the consequence, and what that obliges> | <yes / partly / no> |

## Evidence boundaries

| Claim | Source | Population | Window | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <claim> | <source> | <who, as behaviour and context> | <when> | <limit> |

<If a figure entered your reasoning as pressure rather than as evidence, say so
here. That is a disclosure, not a confession.>

## Confidence

- **Confidence:** <low | moderate | high>
- **Why this word and not the one above it:** <one line>
- **Why this word and not the one below it:** <one line>

## Strongest counter-case

<Write the best argument against your own position, in the words of the person
who holds it. If they would not recognise it as their argument, you have written
a strawman and the position is not yet tested.>

**Why you still hold your position:** <one or two sentences>

## Update condition

| Observation | Direction | Effect on confidence |
|---|---|---|
| <a specific, observable thing> | <if it happens / if it does not> | <moves to low / moderate / high> |

<"More data" is not an observation. Name what would be seen, where, and by when.>

## If this position is wrong

- **Cost:** <what it costs, to whom>
- **Reversibility:** <reversible | costly to reverse | one-way>
- **Earliest point the error becomes visible:** <date or event>

## Gaps

<Everything marked `<unknown>` above, listed as open questions with who could
close them and roughly what it would cost to close each one.>
```

## Quality bar

Before saving, check all six exposures are present. Name any that are missing
rather than filling them in yourself:

| Exposure | Present when |
|---|---|
| Choice | A position is stated as one of the live alternatives, not as a topic |
| Evidence | Each load-bearing claim has a source, population, window, and limit |
| Uncertainty | Confidence is one word, defended against the word above and below it |
| Alternatives | The displaced options are named, and the counter-case is one its holder would recognise |
| Owner | Consequence owner and specialist authority are distinguished |
| Revision trigger | The update condition names an observation, a direction, and an effect |

Tell the learner they can stress-test this with `review-artifact`.
