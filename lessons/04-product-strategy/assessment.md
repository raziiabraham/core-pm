## Part 1 — Scenario

Noted serves three populations. Individual knowledge workers are the largest by count and mostly free. Small teams run shared workspaces and are where most paid conversion happens today. Enterprise team leads are the smallest population, the loudest, and hold the largest contracts. The personal document workspace with AI assistance, file storage, and the public publishing path are live; the team collaboration environment is in design; private draft review and AI-first onboarding are planned. The founder made an earlier sequencing decision that may deserve re-examination. A core dependency loses support in ten weeks.

**Hypothetical development — labelled, not in the case.** Assume the founder's earlier sequencing decision was to build the personal workspace first and defer team collaboration. Assume the founder now proposes reversing it: make team collaboration the centre of the next two quarters and position Noted as "the AI workspace for teams." The founder's argument is that small teams pay, enterprise asks loudest, and individuals are mostly free.

**Central move.** Produce a diagnosis and a bet: name the dynamic and the binding constraint, write the hypothesis chain so each link can fail on its own, make a positioning choice with a named sacrifice, allocate one cycle's portfolio against the constraint, sequence it against the ten-week clock, and write a revision trigger someone else can check.

### Interrogate

Press on the weakest link. Ask "why" at least three times. Use this wording:

1. "Your constraint is 'we do not have team collaboration.' Why is that binding rather than the most requested thing? If it were live tomorrow at no cost, which number moves — and how do you know, given that paid conversion already happens in small teams without it?"
2. "You position for teams. Which population now gets a worse product, and which request with revenue attached will you decline? Why should the account manager believe that, when the enterprise request is also 'a team'?"
3. "Your bet leans on a capability that is in design. Why is that an advantage and not an undefended bet? What is its exercise condition and expiry, and what does the ten-week dependency clock do to your sequence?"

### Held / partly held / did not hold

| Verdict | What it looks like in the learner's answer |
|---|---|
| **Held** | The dynamic is a mechanism a reader can disagree with — for example, value is captured where workspaces are shared but the product is built for one person; or activation is unsegmented so nobody can yet say which population moved, and the diagnosis is marked provisional with the overturning evidence named. The binding-constraint test is run on at least three candidates, one each from the largest, loudest, and paying populations. The hypothesis chain separates small teams from enterprise, defines the audience by behaviour, and names the coupling between value and retention in a collaboration product. The position commits to one audience, a named alternative, a valued difference, and a sustaining capability marked live, in design, or planned — with team collaboration labelled a bet, not an asset. The three sacrifices are stated. The portfolio subtracts Signal 4 as a constraint, funds scale by paid-seat concentration rather than by the 4%, and puts enterprise summaries at zero or a bounded test. The sequence retires the most decision-relevant uncertainty first — whether the paying population converts because of collaboration or for another reason — and checks that the result is visible before the next commitment. The revision trigger has observable, threshold, owner, and response, and is measurable given the 18% identity gap and missing meeting type. The founder's earlier decision is treated as a hypothesis to re-examine, not as authority and not as error. |
| **Partly held** | The diagnosis is sound but the position gives up nothing; "team" merges small teams and enterprise; the bet is built on the in-design capability without the label; the trigger cannot be measured; or the sequence claims learning that will not be visible in time. When pressed, the learner repairs it. |
| **Did not hold** | "Be the AI workspace for teams" is offered as the strategy. Or nothing is sacrificed. Or all four signals are funded. Or the founder's proposal is accepted or rejected on authority rather than diagnosis. |

## Part 2 — Boundary questions

One open question per lesson. Do not accept a restatement of the model as an answer.

**ST-01.** Diagnosis names the binding constraint. When does a true diagnosis stop being useful, and what do you do when the constraint cannot yet be identified?
→ When the constraint sits outside your control — say so, name who controls it, then diagnose the strongest constraint inside your control without pretending it is the main one. When it is not yet identifiable — as with an unsegmented activation decline — write a provisional diagnosis with the discriminating evidence named, and date it.

**ST-02.** The six hypotheses are tested one at a time. When can they not be separated, and what must be fixed first?
→ When value and retention are entangled by network or collaboration effects — a shared workspace with one member has low value because it has one member — test the pair together at a realistic unit, the team. And until the audience is defined by behaviour rather than label, the lower five cannot be stated precisely enough to fail.

**ST-03.** Positioning commits to an audience. When is it legitimate to hold more than one, and what turns a "bridge" into drift?
→ An early product may hold two candidate audiences if the resource split is stated and time-boxed. A bridge audience is legitimate only with a named condition for leaving. And commitments made at different levels of abstraction — tight audience, loose alternative — produce a position that cannot decline anything.

**ST-04.** A portfolio allocates capacity by proportion. What breaks that reasoning?
→ Capacity is not fungible: when the two engineers who can migrate are the only two, a percentage split is fiction — allocate by named people. A hard deadline is not a bet but a constraint removed before allocation. And a portfolio does not tell you the order.

**ST-05.** Sequencing retires uncertainty and preserves options. When does it produce no value, and when is option preservation wrong?
→ When step one's result is not visible before step two commits, you have split work, not sequenced it. Option preservation is wrong when there is no realistic path to exercising the option, or when delay is itself the dominant risk under a dated exposure.

**ST-06.** A strategy narrative lets others apply decision rules. What can communication not repair, and where do decision rules break?
→ It cannot repair a strategy with no diagnosis — a clear narrative spreads a bad choice faster. Rules break outside the cases they were designed for; a rule that declines enterprise-only requests will wrongly kill a request that mostly serves your committed audience. The narrative must say what it does not cover and where those cases go.
