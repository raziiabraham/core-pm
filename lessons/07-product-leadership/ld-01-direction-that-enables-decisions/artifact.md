# Artifact template · Decision-enabling direction

The tutor fills this in with the learner, using the learner's judgment and the
learner's evidence. Never invent a number, a customer, or a quote. Where
something is unknown, write `<unknown>` and carry it into the Gaps section. A
direction built on an invented fact will be applied by someone who cannot tell
which parts you knew.

Delete the guidance in parentheses in the finished artifact.

```markdown
# Direction · <team or product area>

- **Date:** <YYYY-MM-DD>
- **Period this covers:** <start> to <end>
- **Author:** <name or role>
- **Written for:** <the person who will apply this without you in the room>

## Diagnosis

<Three sentences. What situation is this team actually in? Name the constraint
or dynamic that makes this period different from the last one. Not an
aspiration — an assessment.>

## Ordering

<One sentence. When these two things collide, this one wins this period.>

- **What wins:** <the interest that takes precedence>
- **What loses:** <the interest that is displaced — name it, do not soften it>
- **Why, in one line:** <the reason grounded in the diagnosis>

## Constraints for this period

| # | We will not | Applies to a request when | Who can waive it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <constraint stated so a stranger can apply it> | <observable test, not a judgment call> | <person or role> |
| 2 | <constraint> | <test> | <person or role> |

## What this direction does not resolve

<The trade-off classes this direction deliberately leaves to a person. Include
any case where the consequence is one-way, novel, or where two constraints above
would collide. A direction that claims to resolve everything will authorize
something you did not intend.>

- <class> → decided by <person or role>
- <class> → decided by <person or role>

## Worked cases

<Apply the direction to at least two real, live cases. Write the answer it
produces, including one you did not like.>

| Live case | What the direction resolves | Sentence used |
|---|---|---|
| <case> | <the answer> | <which line above> |
| <case> | <the answer, including "it does not resolve this"> | <which line above> |

## Evidence behind the ordering

| Claim the ordering depends on | Source | Population | Window | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <claim> | <source> | <who> | <when> | <limit> |

## Uncertainty

- **Confidence in the ordering:** <low | moderate | high> — <why, in one line>
- **Strongest argument against it:** <the best case for the displaced option>
- **Who on the team disagrees, and with which sentence:** <name the dissent, or
  `<unknown>` if you have not asked>

## Revision trigger

<The observation and threshold that would make this ordering wrong, and who is
watching for it. "We will revisit next quarter" is a calendar entry, not a
trigger.>

- **Observable:** <what is watched>
- **Threshold:** <the number or event that trips it>
- **Owner of the watch:** <person or role>

## Gaps

<Everything marked `<unknown>` above, listed as open questions with who could
close them and by when.>
```

## Quality bar

Before saving, check all six exposures are present. Name any that are missing
rather than filling them in yourself:

| Exposure | Present when |
|---|---|
| Choice | An ordering sentence names both the winner and the displaced interest |
| Evidence | Each claim the ordering rests on has a source, population, window, and limit |
| Uncertainty | Confidence is stated, and the strongest argument against the ordering is written down |
| Alternatives | The worked cases include at least one answer the author did not want |
| Owner | Each unresolved class is assigned to a named person, and constraints say who can waive them |
| Revision trigger | An observable, a threshold, and a person watching for it |

One further test specific to this artifact: hand it to someone who was not in
the room, with two live competing requests. If they answer "it depends — what do
you think?", the ordering section is not finished.

Tell the learner they can stress-test this with `review-artifact`.
