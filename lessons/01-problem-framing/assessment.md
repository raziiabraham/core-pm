## Part 1 — Scenario

Noted's public publishing path is live. A private draft-review workflow is planned, not built. Small teams are where most paid conversion happens today; they run shared workspaces with light collaboration and growing file volume. The support lead has recorded several customer conversations and is closest to what people actually said before it became a summary.

**Hypothetical development — labelled, not in the case.** Assume the support lead tells you that two small-team customers, in separate conversations this month, asked whether Noted will let them get a document reviewed privately before it is published. Assume both are paying customers and the two conversations were about different documents. The support lead's summary is one sentence: "Small teams need draft review before publishing, and it is already on the roadmap." The founder hears this and asks whether the planned workflow should be pulled into this cycle.

**Central move.** Turn this signal into a bounded decision worth investigating: state the observation with no cause attached, name mechanisms that genuinely compete, bound every claim, size the frame to the next choice, draw the audience as a behaviour with an exclusion, and say whether this opportunity earns attention against the four signals already competing for it.

### Interrogate

Press on the weakest link. Ask "why" at least three times. Use this wording:

1. "You said small teams *need* draft review. Two conversations, one channel, one month. Which word in your claim did the evidence not earn — and why did you keep it?"
2. "Both of your mechanisms assume the problem is in the publishing step. Why is that assumption safe? Give me a mechanism that puts the cause somewhere you would rather it was not — in what these teams already do instead, or in what 'private' means to them."
3. "You compared this against the enterprise summaries request. Why is that the right comparison? Why not against the dependency migration — and if not, what does that tell you about what kind of thing each one is?"

### Held / partly held / did not hold

| Verdict | What it looks like in the learner's answer |
|---|---|
| **Held** | The observation is stated without a cause. At least two mechanisms compete: one puts the cause outside the publishing step, or in the workaround the teams use today. The claim carries population (two small-team accounts), window (one month), source (support lead, in conversation), and limit (says nothing about how common this is or whether anyone would pay). The frame is the next choice — pull the planned workflow forward, investigate first, or leave the roadmap — with a date taken from the cycle, not a survey of "collaboration strategy". The audience is a behaviour (publishes documents that others read, wants a check before it is public) with a named exclusion. "Planned" is treated as an ownership fact, not as evidence of need. Signal 4 is subtracted as a constraint before this is ranked. The learner commits and names the observation that would reverse the position. |
| **Partly held** | Most steps run, but one link that carries weight fails: "need" or "already on the roadmap" is used as evidence; the two mechanisms share a buried assumption; "small teams" (a plan tier) stands in for a behaviour; or the frame widens to "how should Noted do collaboration". When pressed, the learner finds and repairs the link without being told what it is. |
| **Did not hold** | The learner starts from the solution and works backwards. Or the observation cannot be stated without a cause. Or the migration is ranked as if it were an opportunity. Or, when pressed, the learner defends two requests as prevalence. Or no position is committed. |

## Part 2 — Boundary questions

One open question per lesson. Do not accept a restatement of the model as an answer.

**PF-01.** Decision-first says the decision comes before the method. When is it wrong to demand a decision statement before work starts, and what does honest work look like there?
→ Genuine exploration — a new market, an unfamiliar user, an area nobody understands — is legitimate without a decision statement, as long as it is labelled as exploration. The failure is presenting exploratory output as if it settled a decision it was never designed to settle.

**PF-02.** When does naming a mechanism before acting stop being worth the effort?
→ Two cases. When the fix is cheap, fast, reversible, and helps under every mechanism you can name — do it and watch. And when the symptom has no single mechanism — split the symptom by population, request type, or time before explaining it. The unit of explanation must match the unit of the symptom.

**PF-03.** Where does the four-field discipline stop being useful, and what does bounding a claim not do?
→ It is for claims that carry a decision, not every sentence; applying it everywhere produces documents nobody reads. And bounded is not sufficient. A claim can carry all four fields and still be too thin to act on. Bounding shows the thinness; it does not repair it.

**PF-04.** Right-sizing pushes toward the next consequential choice. When is the broad frame the correct one?
→ When the decision is one-way, sets a precedent, or the same narrow choice has now recurred — then the pattern is the problem. A frame can also be widened when new evidence arrives, but not silently after the narrow frame produced an answer you disliked.

**PF-05.** Behaviour-and-context boundaries are preferred over attributes. When is an attribute the correct boundary, and when does a behavioural boundary become circular?
→ An attribute is correct when it is the actual constraint: legal, accessibility, data residency, contractual. A behavioural boundary is circular for a genuinely new capability nobody performs yet; there, define by the adjacent behaviour and the condition that makes today's approach costly, and label the target behaviour as hypothesised.

**PF-06.** The five tests rank opportunities. What should not enter the ranking at all, and what do the tests not tell you?
→ Forced choices — a dependency losing support, a legal deadline — are constraints: no real alternative, consequence appears only past a threshold. Subtract their capacity first. Complements that share a mechanism should not be ranked against each other. And passing all five tests says nothing about whether the proposed solution will work.
